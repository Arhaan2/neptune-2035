## Recommended precise numerical and evaluation rules

1. At boundary `t`, apply due events in stable admission sequence and execute existing controller resolution. Advance one canonical step `[t,t+h)` using the actual dt>0 dispatch. Integrate its service shortfall and battery powers exactly once. Thermal duration uses beginning temperatures (left hold); sample endpoint temperatures after thermal advancement for extrema, with their actual endpoint timestamp. No continuous inter-step extrema guarantee is made.
2. Service shortfall is `max(0, requiredAccelerators - canonicalServiceableAccelerators) * h`, in accelerator-seconds. Service limit is met when shortfall is no greater than the persisted numeric tolerance; explicitly name whether duration is based on exact shortfall or this tolerance. A tolerance must never produce a negative shortfall. Module/domain violation durations may be separate; campus any-violation duration is a Boolean union per interval.
3. Thermal bounds need explicit inclusivity. Baseline thermal acceptance uses coolant `>=318.15 K` or air `>=313.15 K` as violated, whereas controller throttling has separate thresholds. Preserve that distinction in defaults; criteria can persist an explicit alternative only if supported. Missing thermal observations are unavailable, not zero K or zero violation.
4. Minimum capacity and maximum temperatures are observed sample extrema including initial/final post-event boundaries; duration/shortfall use `[0,T)`. At a terminal trip, observed minimum may be zero while shortfall and interruption-duration contribution are zero. Explain this combination. Ties should deterministically select earliest time and stable asset order.
5. An interruption episode starts only on a positive-duration violating interval; aggregate count/longest duration stay exact after detailed evidence eviction. Open episode state persists. Separately retain instantaneous boundary violation evidence, especially at T. Do not fabricate a positive-duration episode for simultaneous trip/restore at one boundary.
6. Recovery needs all declared service/thermal predicates continuously for dwell D. Record interruption/reference event, candidate onset and confirmation. Renewed violation resets pending dwell. Final status is based on the latest unresolved observation, not an earlier recovered episode. A new terminal failure prevents a healthy-current-state claim even if an earlier episode had achieved its dwell. Pending onset survives checkpoint/reload. Zero dwell, zero duration, terminal restore and terminal renewed failure require explicit tests.
7. Synthetic arithmetic oracle independently yields shortfall `60*5 + 20*5 = 400 accelerator-seconds`, min 40, service duration 10 s, first violation 5 s, candidate onset 15 s and confirmation 20 s for healthy `[15,20)` and healthy terminal boundary. This calculation does not establish correctness of a real simulator demonstration.
8. Battery discharge is bus energy `integral(P_discharge dt)/3600`; charge is charger input/bus energy `integral(P_charge dt)/3600`. Stored delta is `eta_charge*charge - discharge/eta_discharge`; modeled losses are `discharge*(1/eta_discharge-1) + charge*(1-eta_charge)`. Persist all supported quantities and clear labels; net state-of-charge change cannot replace throughput. Efficiencies are per installed module, so sum after per-module conversion.
9. Warmup predicates must include a continuous interval, a maximum duration, temperature-rate tolerance and specified service/thermal/storage/controller conditions. Battery rate/SOC behavior must be explicit so settling does not hide charging or UPS discharge. Timeout is a distinct failure to obtain a settled start and cannot start a falsely successful evaluation. Warmup diagnostics remain visible; experiment-relative origin is separate from physical clock.
10. Quantities are finite and nonnegative where physically required; signed paired differences and net storage delta are permitted. Use declared tolerances for numerical comparison, not blanket rounding. Imported aggregate values are saved reported values until a deterministic recalculation independently verifies them.
11. Per-run extension should at least persist definition/version identity, run status/reason, origin, warmup state, last accepted step/time, metrics, open violation/recovery state, transition sequence, input event cursor, retained-evidence bounds and coverage/truncation. Duplicate worker messages can replace a checkpoint but must not increment aggregate values. Failed candidate mutation stays in an unpublished clone.
